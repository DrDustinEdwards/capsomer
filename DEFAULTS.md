# Capsomer's defaults

One page. Every line is a default with its reason, not a law: a better design overrides it and records why on the component's page. CI blocks only on accessibility, security, privacy and correctness. The token check warns and never blocks; adding a token is the normal fix.

- **One language, one component per need.** Anything built for one app is usable by all; similar needs use the same component. Why: the apps visibly belong together and nothing is built twice.
- **Ours, never bought.** Other work is a source of ideas only. Base UI is the one UI dependency, for combobox and menu. shadcn/ui (Base UI flavor, nova style, pinned at d75a96ab787f) is the reference for craft, never a source of code. Why: rulings 3 and 5.
- **Plain CSS in layers.** Tokens as custom properties, native nesting, container queries; every Capsomer rule in a `cap.*` layer so an app's own CSS wins. Why: no build step to share, and adoption one screen at a time.
- **Shared building blocks, not copies.** Dialog, listbox, popover and meter are built once; the confirm dialog, detail panel, command menu, shortcuts, select, combobox, menu, tooltip, message and the meter family assemble from them. Why: one open, close, focus and keyboard behavior, and one look, in every overlay and bar.
- **Native first.** `dialog`, the Popover API, `details`, real links and buttons; JavaScript where it is better; Base UI where the platform falls short. Why: less code, and the browser's accessibility.
- **Status is shape, word and color together.** Never color alone. No data is a state with a reason, never a zero.
- **Reversible actions happen at once and offer Undo; one-way actions preview, then perform, in a dialog** with focus on Cancel. A switch only for reversible on/off state.
- **Messages with Undo stay until dismissed**, in an in-page status region; `z` is Undo. No floating toast. Why not shadcn's Sonner: its stack, 4 second auto-dismiss and swipe can hide the one warning or failed Undo the person must not miss (WCAG 2.2.1); the item does wear the popover's surface.
- **Every control is reachable and operable by keyboard**, with a visible focus ring at 3:1. Single-key shortcuts can be turned off.
- **Compact by default, comfortable on request.** `data-density="compact"` (the Portal's values, one step roomier since 0.18: 14px text, 34px controls, 40px rows, 20px cards) or `"comfortable"` (vega's spacing) on the root or any wrapper moves `--control`, `--pad-*`, `--gap`, `--row-h` and `--fs-*`; no component hard-codes a size density should move. Why: dense consoles and roomy pages use the same components.
- **A site that differs keeps it in a site layer, never its own copy.** Its entry in `SITES` (`tokens/sites.mjs`) generates `capsomer/site-<name>.css`; the site imports it after `tokens.css` and `families.css` and puts `data-cap-site="<name>"` on `<html>` or a wrapper. Literal values only; a brand color stays a family. Why: one checked home for each site's differences.
- **Tooltips wait 300 ms (shadcn: 0) and open on keyboard focus only (`:focus-visible`);** the next one opens at once. Why: a pointer crossing a toolbar must not flash tips, and a click must not leave one over a button.
- **Motion explains a change.** 100, 200 or 400 ms on three easings: a long ease-out for arriving and leaving, an ease-in-out for moving, a spring for a release. What leaves goes faster than it came; a press gives at once and springs back; a chosen highlight slides to the new choice, a changed button label morphs, a sheet follows the finger and springs back unless flung away; pages and themes crossfade (a view transition); a palette opened from the keyboard does not move. Nothing moves under reduced motion; text being read never moves. Why: the references the family admires (Arc, Radix Themes, Emil Kowalski's work) feel smooth for these reasons, not for more motion.
- **Text 4.5:1, boundaries 3:1, in both themes, and the APCA floors too**, checked by the palette generator and again as painted. APCA Lc 90 for body text, 75 for accent text and links, 60 for any other text (muted, dim, status words, button labels), 45 for the focus ring, 30 for a control's border (`LEVELS` in `tokens/palette-core.mjs`). Why: WCAG 2.2 is the standard, but its ratio overrates gray on near-black, so dark muted text, buttons, rings and borders passed it at about half their light contrast (the dark mode plan, 2026-10-29).
- **Dark is designed, not inverted.** Every family's dark theme is its own lightness table in OKLCH: a near-black ground in the family's hue (never pure black), lighter surfaces for depth with shadows as the second cue, text a little under white, and accents lighter than in light. Nothing turns dark by swapping two tokens: the manual's signs use `--sign` and `--on-sign`, a dark panel in both themes. A design is drawn and reviewed in both themes at once. Why: the reading research favors dark text on light, so light text on dark needs its full contrast, and the systems people admire (Apple, Material, GitHub, Radix) all build dark as a separate scale.
- **The viewer's "increase contrast" setting is honored.** Under `prefers-contrast: more` every family takes a generated stronger set in both themes (muted text, status words and links at Lc 75, rings at Lc 60, borders at Lc 45, dividers visible), and a site's own neutrals go through the same pass. The Light and Dark switch keeps its two choices; the device's setting turns this on. Why: Apple, Material and Windows all offer it, and it costs no new control.
- **Which step does which job.** The brand color (`--accent`, step 9) only goes where 3:1 is enough (large fills, the logo, and the focus ring in light). Links, headings, the active menu item and accent text use `--accent-text` (step 11), which in dark is lifted to Lc 75 and is also the focus ring there. The primary button fill uses `--primary` (step 10, label `--primary-fg`), moved away from its label until the label reaches Lc 60; its edge is `--primary-line` (the fill, except amber's light theme, where the fill is lightened for its dark label and the edge keeps the 3:1 step 9). Why: step 9 sits at the white-text bar and cannot also be text on a tinted surface (rulings.md, rule 18).
- **A flat image gets `.cap-img-flat`**: an edge, and in dark a little dimming (`--img-filter`) so a white screenshot does not glow. An image drawn for both themes uses `<picture>` with a `(prefers-color-scheme: dark)` source. Photos take neither.
- **Hit areas at least 24 px; 44 px on a touch screen.** Inputs 16 px on a touch screen.
- **Tables scroll on a phone by default**, in a labeled region; cards per table once tested.
- **Every chart is a sentence, a picture and a table.** Enarratio draws the whole figure (the title, one sentence that says what the chart shows with its numbers, the picture, a summary, the source and a CSV link, and the numbers as a table behind "Show data", closed by default and in the HTML either way); the frame (`.cap-chart`) only lays it out in the family's look and redraws it to its width. Why: a picture alone fails the reader who cannot see it (WCAG 1.1.1), a server-rendered page must be readable as delivered by AI agents and search engines, and one figure built in one place cannot drift from a second copy.
- **Chart colors are tokens, never chart code.** Eight series colors (purple, fox and teal first, then sage, indigo, pine, orchid, rust) and a five-step ramp in the family's hue are tokens (`--series-1` to `--series-8`, `--ramp-1` to `--ramp-5`); `css/enarratio.css` maps every `--enarratio-*` property onto them. Each series is at least 3:1 against the surface, ground and raised surface in both themes, every pair is at least 10 CIEDE2000 apart with typical vision and under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia (Enarratio's own bar, below which its `stylesheet()` throws), and no companion sits within 6 of a status color; `npm run check` verifies it. Under "increase contrast" the charts take the family's stronger text, muted text and gridlines (`capsomerMoreTheme` for `stylesheet()`), and under forced colors Enarratio's own system colors, textures and dashes. Why: a chart is read by everyone, and a change to a token must reach every chart.
